# Writing and handoff

Use only the section for the requested format. The defaults below are editorial choices, not proven engagement formulas. Explicit author requirements take precedence. The [research synthesis](research.md) records the evidence and its limitations.

## Short posts and X/Twitter

Give a reader enough context to understand the post without opening a link. Select one observation, useful fact, or conclusion from the source. Put a concrete point before background. An opening can create curiosity by showing a real tension or unexpected detail, but it should tell readers what the subject is and deliver what it promises.

Compress by removing repeated setup, incidental detail, and redundant modifiers. Keep the qualification that makes a claim true. If the qualified claim cannot fit, choose a narrower supported point. Do not convert “in our small trial” into a universal result or drop attribution to save characters. A public source link or brief named attribution may be enough; keep full provenance privately.

As checked on 2026-09-12, X's standard post budget is 280 weighted characters. URLs recognized by X count as 23; emoji sequences and Unicode have special rules. Plain string length is not an X validity check. Use an available platform composer or official `twitter-text` implementation for exact validation, without submitting the post or installing dependencies implicitly. Count simple plain-text drafts directly only when every character's weight is known. Otherwise report the count as unverified and leave room; do not certify that it fits. Include visible numbering and other additions in the final count. Recheck [X counting rules](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/counting-characters) when research is enabled; do not assume access to longer paid posts.

For a requested thread, split at meaningful boundaries. Each part needs a clear subject and one contribution; avoid fragments that depend on a pronoun several posts back. Make order explicit, such as `1/3`, and keep critical qualifications with the claim they constrain. The opening should give value even if nobody continues. End when the source's point is complete, without filler to reach a prescribed thread length.

## LinkedIn feed posts

Use the author's actual observation, decision, or lesson and explain why it matters to readers. Connect paragraphs naturally. A supplied experience may support a story; do not manufacture a failure-to-triumph arc, dialogue, vulnerability, or a universal career lesson.

Put the useful subject and point early enough to make sense in a collapsed preview. There is no fixed “see more” character boundary assumed here; device, layout, and attachments can change what appears. Use paragraph breaks at changes of thought, not after every sentence to create artificial suspense. A short list is appropriate for actual parallel lessons or steps.

As checked on 2026-09-12, the [feed post limit](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176) is 3,000 characters. It is a ceiling, not a length target. Count the final body and state the method; unusual Unicode near the limit needs composer verification or an explicit unverified note. A longer requested feed post needs trimming or the author's agreement to change format. Use plain text without Markdown heading markers or asterisks pretending to be rendered emphasis.

Close with the author's implication, a relevant action, or a specific question that the source naturally raises. Avoid generic “Agree?”, requests to comment a keyword, obligatory tagging, and promises to send resources that do not exist. Link where it helps the reader; do not hide sources in a first comment on the unsupported assumption that links always reduce reach.

## Longform: LinkedIn articles and personal blogs

Use longform when the supplied material needs room for explanation, examples, a counterargument, or reflection. A LinkedIn article and a feed post are distinct surfaces. For a standalone tutorial, researched explainer, Medium piece, or an illustrated article request, use the [article skill](../../article/SKILL.md). For personal blog or LinkedIn longform handled here, reuse the [article guide's source fidelity and metadata principles](../../article/references/articles.md); keep this skill's `post.md` handoff and the requested destination.

Write an accurate title, a concrete opening, and a connected argument. Use headings when they help readers navigate real sections. Introduce unfamiliar concepts, develop the supplied examples, and keep evidence near claims. Let longer paragraphs carry connected reasoning where that improves flow. End with the author's supported conclusion rather than a compulsory recap, inspirational slogan, or sales CTA.

Treat the word target as a budget, not permission to add substance. If the source supports only a brief reflection, deliver that with a private length note. Do not expand by inventing scenes or repeating the point in multiple forms. A personal essay may stay personal; accessibility does not mean flattening its voice into textbook prose.

For LinkedIn articles, use its [article editor guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a522427) for current formatting and preview behavior when needed. Deliver transferable text, descriptive links, and any actual requested media with captions and alternatives. Do not promise lossless Markdown import. For blogs, preserve the requested Markdown/frontmatter conventions and established links. Do not add a promotional feed teaser unless requested.

## Accessibility and final edit

Use ordinary text, not Unicode characters imitating bold or italic fonts. Hashtags are optional; include only relevant ones, put multiword tags in `#TitleCase`, and keep them out of sentence grammar where possible. Use emoji sparingly when they fit the author's voice; the words must carry the meaning without them. Avoid decorative rows and repeated emoji bullets.

If visuals are requested or materially useful, read the shared [visual guide](../../write/references/visuals.md). Supply descriptive alt text based on the actual image, captions where needed, and textual equivalents for information carried in graphics. Do not fabricate screenshots or documentary proof. Inspect the destination preview when available without submitting it, and report unavailable rendering checks honestly.

Read the draft aloud for mechanical rhythm, unexplained transitions, and manufactured excitement. Apply the shared editorial punctuation pass to all newly written prose. Protect literal quotations, code, URLs, numbers, and author-specified wording while editing.
