# Research on source-based social writing

Research checked on 2026-09-12. This synthesis covers public-facing short posts, threads, LinkedIn feed posts and articles, and personal blogs. It separates platform requirements, empirical findings, and editorial choices. Living platform pages need rechecking when their rules affect a deliverable. Writing advice does not substantiate the facts of a particular post.

## Preserve a real contribution

LinkedIn's current help guidance allows AI assistance with language, proofreading, and concision while asking members to retain their own perspective, voice, and experience. It recommends reviewing and approving assisted content and explaining heavy AI use when it is not apparent. This is a platform recommendation about transparency, not a declaration that all assisted writing requires identical disclosure text. The skill therefore starts from substantive author material and records the actual assistance. [^1]

LinkedIn also says it is reducing distribution of generic content lacking a clear perspective. Its June announcement reports internal detection results, but supplies no independent evaluation that would let a writing skill predict an individual post's treatment. Removing a punctuation mark cannot establish originality or guarantee reach. The useful editorial decision is to retain the author's concrete contribution rather than replace it with interchangeable observations. [^2]

A source requirement must scale with the output. A complete thought with its context can support a short post. An extended reflection needs enough author material to sustain an argument. Existing third-party reporting can support an attributed summary, but it cannot justify writing “I discovered” or inventing the author's opinion. This source gate is Copywriter's design choice for fidelity; the research does not establish that every social post must originate in a formal outline.

## Make the point easy to find

Nielsen Norman Group's 2020 synthesis describes five eye-tracking studies spanning 13 years, more than 500 participants, and over 750 hours of observation. Readers commonly scanned, with reading depth affected by motivation, task, focus, and personal habits. Clear headings, front-loaded information, and plain language supported finding relevant material. The studies concern web reading, not a controlled comparison of X or LinkedIn post performance. [^3]

The practical application is to introduce the subject and relevance early, use coherent paragraphs, and label substantial sections in longform. This does not justify one sentence per paragraph, a mandatory list, or the claim that nobody reads long articles. A reader seeking a detailed explanation has a different task from someone browsing a feed. Preserve depth when the source and task warrant it.

The older 1997 NN/G study compared versions of a website and measured task usability, including time, errors, memory, and structure. Concise, scannable, and less promotional writing improved those measures. Its percentages are not estimates of sales conversion or social engagement and should not be reused as such. [^4]

## Offer useful substance and honest interest

Berger and Milkman's 2012 paper combined an observational study of nearly 7,000 New York Times articles with experiments on emotion and sharing. Practical utility, interest, and surprise were positively associated with sharing; high-arousal emotion mattered beyond simply positive versus negative tone. The author-hosted PDF is an ahead-of-print version. Newspaper email sharing and laboratory transmission are not today's personalized social feeds. [^5]

The defensible writing application is to surface a supplied useful detail, discovery, or consequence. A surprising fact can make an opening engaging when it is true and contextualized. The findings do not authorize invented emotional stories, outrage, anxiety, or promises of virality. A source without a dramatic event does not need one added.

LinkedIn's Page posting guide favors original perspective, actionable material, and conversations with readers. It also encourages testing posting times for the audience. It is guidance for organizational Pages, without a disclosed causal study behind every performance claim. Its frequency advice is not a universal mandate for individual authors. [^6]

For this skill, invitations to respond are optional. A focused question can extend the supplied argument, but forced questions and unrelated CTAs weaken the handoff. Choosing a relevant audience and goal is more useful than mechanically applying a hook-body-CTA template. These are editorial judgments, not experimentally proven wording formulas.

## Distinguish formats and enforce real limits

X documents weighted counting for a standard 280-character post: detected URLs count as 23, and emoji and Unicode need special treatment. It recommends its `twitter-text` library for accurate counts. This makes plain string length an unreliable general validator. A strict fit claim requires a suitable counter or a draft whose individual weights can be checked directly. Longer account-specific features should not be assumed. [^7]

LinkedIn documents a 3,000-character feed-post ceiling and directs longer pieces to its article publishing surface. Its article editor supports a separate title, links, media, and formatting. These sources establish capabilities, not an ideal length or a guaranteed preview cutoff. [^8][^9]

The skill therefore treats single posts, threads, and articles as different deliverables. Shortening should select a narrower supported point while preserving the qualification that makes it true. Threads need meaningful parts and visible order. Longform needs enough source substance for development. A hard limit should not be satisfied by quietly removing a caveat or changing the requested format.

## Keep formatting accessible and natural

Defra's 2022 social accessibility guidance recommends clear language, explicit thread order, capitalized words in hashtags, care with emoji, ordinary fonts, and meaningful alternatives for media. It explains how decorative characters and delayed key information can make reading with assistive technology harder. Its platform-specific interface details may age; the underlying accessibility concerns remain useful. [^10]

Apply those concerns with readable paragraphs, optional relevant hashtags, text that carries meaning without emoji, and real alt text for selected visuals. Reserve headings and lists for actual structure. Accessibility is a reason to avoid Unicode pseudo-fonts, not a reason to remove all personality or useful emphasis.

Copywriter's punctuation preference is a house style requested by the plugin maintainer. Prefer periods, commas, and ordinary sentence construction; avoid em dashes and repeated theatrical separators in newly drafted prose. Also discourage formulaic contrast lines, decorative headings, repeated bold labels, and compulsory cliffhangers. These choices are not a scientific classifier of AI authorship. Preserve quotations, syntax, meaningful hyphens, locale conventions, and explicit author choices.

## Claims the evidence does not settle

The reviewed sources do not establish a universal optimal word count, posting hour, number of hashtags, or opening formula. Page advice and creator advice can serve different populations and objectives. A hard platform limit is more authoritative for fit than a marketer's suggested range; a current official rule takes precedence over an old interface walkthrough.

Do not infer that external links always suppress reach, that links belong in comments, or that a punctuation change bypasses detection. LinkedIn explicitly identifies engagement pods and automated comments as inauthentic activity; drafting copy does not authorize either practice or ordinary publication. [^11]

If asked to improve performance using supplied analytics, compare outcomes relevant to the stated goal, such as useful replies or source-link visits. Account history, topic, distribution, timing, and audience differences can confound comparisons. Changing one editorial feature across comparable posts can inform future choices, but a small before/after result is not causal proof. Analytics collection, experimentation, and posting remain outside this drafting skill unless requested.
